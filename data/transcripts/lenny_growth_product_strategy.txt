Product strategy is best when it starts with a real user problem and not a clever pitch. The strongest teams talk to customers early, write down what they say, and look for patterns in the frustration. The product is not an opinion; it is a system that solves a specific, observable bottleneck.

When we talk about product-market fit, I do not think of one magical moment. I think of a loop: you listen to users, ship a small experiment, watch how they behave, and then keep learning. The loop compounds. A weak company waits for clarity. A strong company creates more clarity by making more observations.

Distribution is product. If no one can find the value, the value does not matter enough yet. Growth is not just acquisition channels; it is the way product decisions change what the user sees and repeats. The most durable growth engines come from sharing a product experience that is easy to understand and easy to recommend.

If you want to build a serious moat, start by learning how users talk about the problem differently than the team talks about it. The gap is where the product opportunity lives.
